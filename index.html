Paper Overview

Abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has become an effective approach for improving mathematical reasoning in large language models. However, a fundamental question remains: when a model fails to solve a problem, does it truly lack the underlying capability? ReSI-Math shows that direct failure does not necessarily imply inability. Some reasoning failures can be recovered by exploring the model's own trajectories, exposing a recoverable capability frontier: a policy-dependent region where solutions are not reliably generated through direct decoding but can be elicited through trajectory-level search.

ReSI-Math uses Self-Judge-guided evolutionary search to identify recoverable failures under a fixed policy and adaptively selects frontier problems for sequential RLVR as the policy evolves. On Qwen3-VL-8B, correct–incorrect trajectory crossover produces 272 verifier-confirmed correct offspring from 621 attempts (43.8%). After three sequential one-shot RLVR stages, the average performance on MATH500 and WE-MATH improves from 78.71 to 80.16.

Core Idea

Failure is not always inability.

ReSI-Math separates what a policy can reliably express from what can still be recovered through bounded search over its own trajectories.

Recoverable Capability Frontier

A problem can be difficult under direct generation while still admitting a verifier-confirmed solution after structured trajectory recombination. This creates a policy-dependent frontier between directly expressed capability, recoverable capability, and currently unexplored capability.

Methodology

A four-stage recursive improvement loop

ReSI-Math decomposes capability exploration and learning into Elicit, Repair, Consolidate, and Reprofile.

01

Elicit

Probe the current policy with multiple direct rollouts and use a rule-based verifier to identify correct and failed trajectories.

  • Fixed-policy trajectory sampling
  • Verifier-confirmed C/F partition
  • Localize candidate frontier regions
02

Repair

Hold model parameters fixed and test whether failed reasoning can be transformed into correct reasoning through Self-Judge-guided evolutionary search.

  • Blind / Guided Self-Judge
  • C–F and F–F crossover
  • Verifier-confirmed failure-to-success transitions
03

Consolidate

Convert recovered capability signals into parameter-level learning through dual-signal RLVR.

  • Frontier-guided top-1 problem selection
  • Single-problem Solver-RLVR
  • Verifier-labeled Judge-RLVR supervision
04

Reprofile

Re-evaluate the candidate pool after each policy update because the most informative frontier problem changes as the policy evolves.

  • Re-sample candidate problems
  • Update success profiles
  • Select the next frontier target
